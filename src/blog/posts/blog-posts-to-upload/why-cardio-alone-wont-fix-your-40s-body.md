## The Treadmill Trap

Most guys over 40 respond to weight gain the same way: add more cardio. More miles, more sweat, more time on the treadmill. It feels productive. It's also usually the wrong lever.

Cardio burns calories while you're doing it. Strength training changes what your body does when you're **not** doing it — 24 hours a day, every day, for the rest of your life.

## What's Actually Changing In Your 40s

Testosterone drifts down. Recovery slows. Muscle mass — the tissue that drives your resting metabolic rate — starts eroding by default if you don't fight for it. Think of muscle as your engine's displacement. A bigger engine burns more fuel at idle. Shrink the engine, and you idle cooler no matter how many hills you run.

This is why the guy who was lean at 30 doing three runs a week finds the same routine isn't cutting it at 45. The engine got smaller. The old fuel plan doesn't match the new engine.

## The Fix Isn't More Cardio — It's Rebuilding The Engine

1. **Prioritize resistance training 3-4x/week.** Compound lifts first — squat, hinge, press, pull. This is the stimulus that tells your body "keep the muscle."
2. **Eat enough protein to actually rebuild.** Most men chronically undereat protein relative to what's needed to preserve muscle in a deficit.
3. **Use cardio as a tool, not a punishment.** Zone 2 work supports recovery and heart health — it's not your primary fat-loss lever anymore.

## The Bottom Line

You're not broken. Your metabolism didn't betray you. It's responding exactly the way biology says it will when muscle mass and hormones shift and the training doesn't shift with them.

Fix the engine, and the rest follows.
