## Key Takeaways

- Your body runs like an engine. Some engines want race fuel. Some want diesel. Put in the wrong fuel and the engine stalls.
- There are 5 types of engines. We call them **neurotypes**. Type 1A, 1B, 2A, 2B, and 3.
- Most guys over 35 have been running the wrong fuel for years. That's why old programs stopped working.

## What Is Neurotype Training?

Every engine has a redline.

Push a diesel truck engine like a race car and it won't go faster. It will break.

Your body has a redline too. Your brain and nerves control how much training you can handle. Some people can lift heavy all day. Some people burn out fast on heavy weight but can do endless reps. Some people need a strict plan or they fall apart.

This is called your **neurotype**. It's not a personality test. It's a training map.

There are 5 neurotypes. Once you know yours, you stop guessing. You train the engine you actually have — not the one your buddy has, not the one on Instagram.

## The Five Neurotypes

### Type 1A — The Intensity Seeker

**One word:** Intensity
**The line:** "I get bigger by getting stronger."

**Who you are:** Competitive. Loud. You want the heaviest plate on the bar and you don't care who's watching. You're a grinder. You can push a heavy weight even when it looks stuck.

**What this means for training:** You run on heavy weight, not lots of reps. Your engine burns out fast if the session runs too long. Keep it under 40 minutes.

**Do this:**
- Heavy weight, low reps
- Big pauses at the bottom of a lift
- Dead-stop starts (like a pin press)

**Skip this:**
- High-rep "burn" sets
- Jump training
- Long sessions

**The mistake we see after 35:** Guys keep adding more sets because "that's what the plan said." Your nervous system can't recover from that much heavy work. Less volume, same weight, better results.

### Type 1B — The Explosive Athlete

**One word:** Explosiveness
**The line:** "I get bigger by moving fast."

**Who you are:** A multitasker. You get bored fast. You move weight with speed, not grinding. You like switching between exercises.

**What this means for training:** You need speed. Slow, grinding reps kill your performance. Pairing two exercises back to back (like a bicep curl, then a tricep push) keeps your brain switched on.

**Do this:**
- Jump training
- Fast, explosive reps
- Pairs of exercises (A1, then A2)

**Skip this:**
- Slow tempo reps
- Grinding through a stuck rep
- One long, boring set after another

**The mistake we see after 35:** Guys slow down their training because they think "slow and controlled" is safer as you age. For a 1B, that kills your results. You still need speed — just smarter speed, with better warm-ups.

### Type 2A — The Chameleon

**One word:** Variation
**The line:** "Everything works. Nothing works for long."

**Who you are:** Right in the middle. Half heavy-lifter, half bodybuilder. You get bored doing the same thing. You need a mix.

**What this means for training:** You handle the most total training volume of any type — but only if you mix heavy work and pump work in the same session. New tools (chains, bands, different bars) keep you motivated.

**Do this:**
- Heavy lift, then lighter pump work, same session
- New equipment often
- Regular program changes

**Skip this:**
- Months of the exact same program
- Only heavy work, or only pump work

**The mistake we see after 35:** Guys stick with one style for too long because "consistency is king." For a 2A, sameness is the problem. You need planned variety, not chaos.

### Type 2B — The Sensation Seeker

**One word:** Sensation
**The line:** "I get stronger by getting bigger."

**Who you are:** You train by feel. You want to feel the muscle working — the pump. You're emotional about your training. You get stronger as a side effect of building muscle, not the other way around.

**What this means for training:** You recover fast from lots of reps but burn out on heavy, max-effort lifting. Bodybuilding-style training is your best friend.

**Do this:**
- Higher reps (6+)
- Supersets and dropsets
- Lighter weight, more volume

**Skip this:**
- Very heavy weight for long stretches
- Low reps as your main diet

**The mistake we see after 35:** Guys chase heavy numbers because that's what "real training" looks like online. For a 2B, that's the wrong fuel. Build the muscle. Strength follows.

### Type 3 — The Controller

**One word:** Control
**The line:** "Building muscle is about controlling stress."

**Who you are:** You didn't grow up loving the gym. You like routine. Surprises stress you out. You want to feel in control of every rep.

**What this means for training:** You need a plan and you need to stick to it. Long warm-ups matter — they calm your nervous system before the real work starts. Slow, controlled reps work best.

**Do this:**
- Same core exercises for a long stretch
- Long, thorough warm-ups
- Slow tempo, pauses

**Skip this:**
- Constant program changes
- Rushed warm-ups
- Hype-yourself-up lifting

**The mistake we see after 35:** Guys jump program to program chasing "the next best thing." For a Type 3, that's the actual cause of their stall. Pick a plan. Stay on it. Let your body trust it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is my neurotype?**
It's the way your brain and nerves respond to training. It tells you how much weight, how many reps, and how much rest your body actually wants.

**Can I be more than one type?**
Yes. Most people are a mix, leaning toward one type. A quiz gives you a starting point. It's not the full picture.

**Does my neurotype change as I get older?**
Your wiring stays the same. But your hormones change what that wiring can do. That's why a plan that worked at 28 can stop working at 45 — even if your type never changed.

**How is this different from a personality test?**
A personality test tells you how you act with people. This tells you how your body reacts to training stress. Two different things.
